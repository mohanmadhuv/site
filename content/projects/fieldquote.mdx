For AGIV's Hackers & Healers Hackathon, my team built FieldQuote, a dictation tool for family medicine preceptors.

<Media caption="A preceptor debrief after a clinic session" />

## Learning from Preceptors

A preceptor supervising a half-day clinic gives residents verbal feedback after nearly every patient. Although it is specific, it's also quick and delivered in the moment. Neither they nor the residents write it down anywhere to keep and return to. Under time pressure, it gets skipped and the resident loses the feedback they could have learned from.

<blockquote className="type-caption leading-[1.618] mx-0 my-[16px]"><span>“Family physicians report spending on average 10 to 19 hours per week on administrative tasks.”</span><span className="block text-right">— Dr. Sarah Cook, Family Physician (CBC)</span></blockquote>

FieldQuote doesn't ask preceptors to write feedback differently, it removes that step completely by introducing dictation. They pick the resident, speak the debrief the way they'd already convey it to the resident, and the dictation comes out as structured feedback the resident receives right away.

Most of the other 44 teams at the hackathon built something preceptors would have to adopt: another inbox, another portal, another tool with its own learning curve stacked on an already overloaded clinic day. FieldQuote fits inside a step that already exists, so there's nothing new to learn.
