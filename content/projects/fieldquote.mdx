For AGIV's Hackers & Healers Hackathon, my team built FieldQuote, a dictation tool for family medicine preceptors. My part was the piece that turns a spoken debrief into feedback and learning materials (Flashcads, quizzes, and more) the resident can actually use.

<Media caption="A preceptor debrief after a clinic session" />

## Why it's different

A preceptor supervising a half-day clinic gives residents verbal feedback after nearly every patient. It's quick and specific, delivered in the moment. They don't write it down anywhere the resident can keep and return to. Under time pressure, that step gets skipped, and the resident loses the feedback.

FieldQuote doesn't ask preceptors to do that second step differently. It removes it. They pick the resident, speak the debrief the way they'd already say it out loud, and the dictation comes out as structured feedback the resident receives right away.

Most of the other 44 teams at the hackathon built something preceptors would have to adopt: another inbox, another portal, another tool with its own learning curve stacked on an already overloaded clinic day. FieldQuote fits inside a step that already exists, so there's nothing new to learn.
